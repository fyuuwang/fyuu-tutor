# Language tutoring pipeline

Use when success is defined by understanding or producing a target language in real communication.

## Flow

1. Define the target situations and required listening, speaking, reading, or writing evidence.
2. Acquire source material without changing the original.
3. Extract and structure vocabulary, expressions, dialogues, exercises, and source anchors through the shared material pipeline.
4. Configure the target writing or pronunciation system, audio strategy, and authoritative overrides in the private project.
5. Map source material and language functions to lessons before generating them.
6. Teach new forms in meaningful context, then require active recall and production.
7. Pair source-faithful examples with changed-context variants; do not replace the source entirely with invented material.
8. Validate source coverage, target-form coverage, pronunciation or writing hooks, active production, links, and output structure.
9. Re-test weak forms after delay and in a different situation before marking them Stable.

Prefer active production, listening discrimination, correction, and scenario response over answer-reveal flashcards. Reading recognition does not prove spoken or written production.

Keep the language, learner goals, source sections, lesson duration, target forms, pronunciation authority, audio strategy, and assessment emphasis in the private project.

Optional language-specific programs live under `scripts/pipelines/language/`. They are implementations for that language, not requirements of the general language pipeline.
